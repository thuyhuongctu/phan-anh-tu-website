Vice President · School of Economics · Can Tho University

Phan Anh Tu

I am Vice President of the School of Economics at Can Tho University, with 27 years of academic experience in international business, entrepreneurship, and institutional economics. My research focuses on Vietnam and the Mekong Delta, where institutional volatility creates distinct constraints and opportunities for firms and entrepreneurs.

My work spans two complementary streams: how institutional context, national culture distance, and firm characteristics shape FDI entry-mode decisions and internationalization–performance relationships, and how entrepreneurs navigate weak institutions, informal economy dynamics, and corruption. It has appeared in Scopus/SSCI journals including Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice, European Management Review, and the Asian Academy of Management Journal.

I have supervised 2 completed PhD dissertations and more than 30 master's theses, and my research on entrepreneurship in the Mekong Delta has been funded by the Vietnamese Ministry of Education and Training. As curriculum architect, I lead programme design aligned with OBE and AUN-QA standards.

Research

My research advances two complementary streams at the intersection of international business and institutional economics. See also my ORCID profile and full CV.

Research statement

1 · Internationalization & foreign direct investment

I investigate how institutional context, national culture distance, and firm characteristics shape FDI entry-mode decisions and internationalization–performance relationships in emerging and transition economies. This work draws on the OLI paradigm, internalization theory, the Uppsala model, and Born-Global/Springboard perspectives. I focus empirically on Vietnam and the Mekong Delta, where institutional volatility and weak rule of law create distinct constraints and opportunities.

2 · Entrepreneurship under institutional constraint

My second stream examines how entrepreneurs navigate weak institutions, informal economy dynamics, and corruption. I explore what motivates bribery, how informal payments affect performance, and how institutional quality shapes entrepreneurial intention and value creation.

Both streams are published in Scopus/SSCI journals including Entrepreneurship Theory and Practice, European Management Review, and the Asian Academy of Management Journal. I have supervised 2 PhD dissertations and 30+ master's theses, and have been funded by the Vietnamese Ministry of Education and Training for research on entrepreneurship in the Mekong Delta.

Teaching

27 years of academic experience with rigorous supervision across undergraduate, master, and PhD levels.

Teaching philosophy

My teaching is grounded in three interlocking commitments: analytical rigor, contextual relevance, and student-centered mentorship.

Analytical rigor

Training students to apply formal frameworks to real phenomena. I emphasize disciplined thinking: What is the research question? What is the evidence? What assumptions are we making? What are the limitations?

Contextual relevance

Situating business and economic theory in the Vietnam and Mekong Delta context. Why does FDI strategy differ across institutional contexts? How do entrepreneurial intentions and corruption interact in emerging economies?

Student-centered mentorship

Meeting students where they are and helping them move toward publication-readiness and an independent scholarly voice. I provide constructive feedback, model the writing process, and create opportunities for collaboration.

Across all levels, I emphasize continuous learning, intellectual humility, and the interconnection between teaching and research.
